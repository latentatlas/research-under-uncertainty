\begin{abstract}
External decision records can reproduce a model's action while leaving their
diagnostic value unresolved. We examine record availability, correspondence,
rule sensitivity and added information in three predecessor panels and a
48-assignment follow-up separating record requirements from external execution.
The follow-up has 46 completed trials and two retained technical losses.
When records were required but model proposals were executed directly,
Astra and Sol each produced eight valid, proposal-matching records; both
supported rules selected the same action in every such record. Sol's eight
matching records included two conditional loss labels. Thus observed
correspondence coexisted with rule invariance and did not ensure continuation
feasibility. In the external-executor condition all completed proposals already
matched the selector, leaving correction of conflicting proposals unmeasured.
All follow-up P/L changes occurred in two continuous-control histories; the
purposeful histories and repeats support descriptive comparisons only.
The predecessor transfer adapter supported no complete pairs. In its separate
32-trial common-menu successor, native actions and rule identity determined
all 32 observed-rule cells. Native actions plus a single-distinct-row check
determined 29 of 32 supported-rule-family cells; a full per-record scan
determined all 32 by construction of the contract. One pilot conclusion
changed under a native-compatible selector completion. These cases show why
an analyst must separate availability, correspondence and sensitivity before
attributing information to a crossing. Task labels reuse a bound menu census
and retain numerical/runtime premises; full task success, internal faithfulness
and improvement in independently scored diagnosis are unmeasured.
\end{abstract}
