# Research question and design choices

An agent can achieve a goal using an incomplete account of its environment. It can also describe a plausible account without choosing experiments that distinguish it from alternatives. This project studies how experimental choices, observed evidence, explicit predictions and task outcomes relate to one another.

The participant receives an interface and a goal, then chooses how to investigate an unfamiliar synthetic system. The research archive links each permitted observation to the next submitted decision. The developer and analyst have access to evaluation information that the participant does not receive.

## Building a difficulty ladder

The aim is a fair range of difficulty that can eventually support comparisons across models and reasoning settings. A useful ladder must do more than make a strong model fail once. Its ordering should persist across matched starting conditions and repeated runs, with easier and harder levels tested under equivalent budgets.

Development versions are used to find candidate levels. Once selected, those levels need testing on fresh conditions. Repeated observations from the same small set of conditions do not establish population performance or a general ranking of models. The present aggregate snapshot is one step in that development process.

Participants may use any valid strategy allowed by the task. A shortcut that legitimately solves the task is evidence about the task design. It is not penalized for failing to resemble the researcher's preferred method.

## Separating completion from learning

The current task score measures whether the participant meets the stated goal. It does not require an exact verbal or executable reconstruction of the environment.

A later measurement version is being prepared to evaluate submitted predictions on additional tests. That version will report task completion and predictive generalization separately. It has not been inserted into the calibration summarized here, and the historical episodes have not been assigned retrospective learning scores.

## What the work contributes so far

The current work combines matched task settings, explicit resource accounting, preserved unsuccessful attempts and a traceable internal record of decisions and outcomes. The published summary explains these design decisions and reports bounded observations. The broader claim of a validated difficulty ladder remains open.
